# Contributor Covenant Code of Conduct

## Our pledge

We pledge to make participation in Vardiel welcoming, safe, and equitable. We value good-faith contributions regardless of experience, background, identity, language, location, or role.

## Expected behavior

Community members are expected to:

- communicate with kindness, honesty, and technical focus;
- respect different viewpoints and levels of experience;
- give and receive constructive feedback;
- acknowledge mistakes and repair harm;
- protect confidential and personal information;
- credit sources and respect software licenses;
- prioritize user safety when discussing operational or security-sensitive behavior.

## Unacceptable behavior

The following are not acceptable in project spaces:

- harassment, threats, stalking, or continued unwanted contact;
- personal attacks, insults, discriminatory language, or demeaning conduct;
- sexualized language or imagery unrelated to the project;
- publishing another person's private information without permission;
- impersonation or attempts to evade moderation;
- deliberate disruption, spam, or unrelated commercial promotion;
- knowingly submitting plagiarized, malicious, or deceptively generated material;
- pressuring someone to disclose private prompts, credentials, security reports, or personal data.

Technical disagreement is not itself misconduct. Critique the proposal, evidence, or code rather than the person.

## Scope

This code applies to issues, pull requests, reviews, repository discussions, project chat, release coordination, and other spaces where someone represents Vardiel.

## Reporting

Report a possible violation privately to a repository maintainer using a private contact method on their GitHub profile. If no private method is available, open a minimal issue requesting a private contact channel without naming people or including sensitive details.

Security vulnerabilities must use [SECURITY.md](SECURITY.md), not the conduct-reporting process.

Maintainers will handle reports as confidentially as practical, avoid conflicts of interest, and give affected people an opportunity to provide relevant context.

## Enforcement

Depending on severity, frequency, and impact, maintainers may use:

1. a private clarification or warning;
2. temporary limits on participation;
3. removal of content or access;
4. a permanent ban from project spaces.

Serious conduct may skip earlier steps. Retaliation against a reporter or participant in an investigation is prohibited.
